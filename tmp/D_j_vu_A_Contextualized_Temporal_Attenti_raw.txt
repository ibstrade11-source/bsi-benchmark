Based on the provided abstract, here is a concise and factual analysis of the article's main claims and contributions.

### **Core Problem**
Existing sequential recommender systems primarily focus on the transitional structure of a user's historical actions. However, they largely ignore **temporal dynamics** (when an action occurred) and **contextual information** (how an action occurred) when predicting a user's next action. 

### **Main Claims**
* **Dynamic Historical Influence:** The influence of past user actions on their current behavior is not static; it fluctuates based on elapsed time and changing contexts.
* **Context-Driven Temporal Weighting:** Effective sequential recommendation requires dynamically calibrating self-attention mechanisms by factoring in the specific "when" and "how" of historical actions alongside the "what."

### **Key Contributions**
1. **Contextualized Temporal Attention Mechanism ("Déjà vu"):** A novel attention-based framework designed to weigh the influence of historical actions using action type, time, and context.
2. **Parameterized Kernel Functions:** The model deploys multiple parameterized kernel functions to capture diverse, complex temporal dynamics over time.
3. **Contextual Kernel Selection:** It utilizes contextual information as a gating mechanism to determine which temporal reweighing kernel to apply to each input.
4. **Empirical Validation:** The authors demonstrate the model's effectiveness through empirical evaluations on two large public datasets, where it consistently outperformed existing state-of-the-art sequential recommendation baselines.
