Based on the provided abstract, here is a concise and factual analysis of the article's main claims and contributions.

### **Main Claims**
* **Limitation of Existing Models:** While local attention mechanisms (like Neighborhood Attention [NA] or Swin Transformer's Shifted Window) reduce the quadratic complexity of self-attention, they weaken the model's ability to capture long-range dependencies and global receptive fields.
* **Efficacy of DiNA:** Dilated Neighborhood Attention (DiNA) can expand receptive fields exponentially and capture global context at no additional computational cost.
* **Synergy of Local and Global Attention:** Local attention (NA) and sparse global attention (DiNA) are complementary; combining them in a single architecture yields superior performance compared to using either alone.
* **Superior Efficiency and Performance:** The proposed DiNAT architecture is faster and more accurate than strong baselines, including NAT, Swin, and ConvNeXt.

### **Key Contributions**
* **New Attention Mechanism (DiNA):** Introduces *Dilated Neighborhood Attention*, a flexible and efficient extension of NA that introduces dilation to capture wider, sparse global context.
* **New Architecture (DiNAT):** Introduces the *Dilated Neighborhood Attention Transformer*, a novel hierarchical vision transformer built by combining NA and DiNA.
* **State-of-the-Art (SOTA) Performance:** Without relying on extra data, the large DiNAT variant set new SOTA benchmarks across multiple computer vision tasks:
  * **COCO:** 58.5 PQ (Panoptic Segmentation), +1.6% box AP (Object Detection), and +1.4% mask AP (Instance Segmentation) over Swin.
  * **ADE20K:** 49.4 PQ (Panoptic Segmentation), 35.4 AP (Instance Segmentation), and +1.4% mIoU (Semantic Segmentation) over Swin.
  * **Cityscapes:** 45.1 AP (Instance Segmentation).
  * **Competitive Segmentation:** Matches SOTA on ADE20K semantic segmentation (58.1 mIoU) and ranks second on Cityscapes semantic segmentation (84.5 mIoU).
